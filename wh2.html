Course Philosophy

The course follows the current Khan Academy World History sequence: Unit 1 Beginnings–600 BCE; Unit 2 600 BCE–600 CE; Unit 3 600–1450; Unit 4 1450–1750 Renaissance and Reformation; Unit 5 1750–1900 Enlightenment and Revolution; Unit 6 the 20th century. The first course ends at 1600, so Unit 4 is deliberately divided between the two courses; the second course begins with the continuation of the 1450–1750 material and proceeds through Units 5 and 6.

Khan supplies the chronological spine. Easy Peasy supplies the lesson/assignment style. VMS supplies the research, note-taking, book-study, essay, and final-paper architecture. The supplied Vedic, devotional, and creationist texts are treated as identifiable sources with their own frameworks, not silently merged into a single “neutral” narrative.

  • Class with teacher / discussion: introduce the historical problem, map, image, document, or lecture.
  • Read / Watch / Listen: complete the assigned Khan lesson and source reading or video packet.

  • Written work: complete-sentence questions, paragraph, chart/map, essay work, presentation, or research notes.
  • Timeline / Map: add the day’s date/period, place, and one causal/network connection.

Grading and Written-Work Rules

This course follows the writing-intensive structure of Easy Peasy and the staged research structure of the VMS class schedule. Traditional chapter tests are not the primary assessment. Daily written work, source analysis, paragraphs, essays, charts, maps, oral presentations, book/source notes, and the cumulative timeline demonstrate mastery.

All essays require a rough draft, proofreading, and final draft. The VMS standard is a minimum of five paragraphs; the final research paper is a substantially longer, footnoted paper with bibliography.

Assignment TypeGrading StructureTotal
Sentence answer1 point for a complete sentence that restates the question; 1 point for content2 points
Paragraph2 points for form (introduction/main idea, body, conclusion); 2 points for clarity and flow; 6 points for content and details10 points
Essay5 points for the introduction paragraph (including thesis); 5 points for the conclusion (restatement of thesis + "why?" and "so what?"); 10 points for each middle paragraph40 points
LEQ / Research checkpointScored on thesis, evidence use, source analysis, and chronological accuracy40 points
Cumulative checkpoint40-point essay or LEQ + source analysis + map/timeline audit + correction log; revise lowest-scoring earlier assignment40+ points

Long-Form Reading and Research Program

Follow the VMS model: students should keep detailed notes on every lecture/video and complete seven substantial books or teacher-approved long-form source works across at least three topic categories. Begin each book 2–3 weeks before it is due. Keep a running digital bibliography and source notebook for the final research paper.

Political philosophy: Selections from Dialectic Spiritualism; a teacher-approved Enlightenment text.

Science / origins: Origins: Creation or Evolution; a modern history/philosophy of science work.

Vedic history / culture: Proof of Vedic Culture’s Global Existence; Advancements of Ancient India’s Vedic Culture.

Jagannatha / bhakti: Jaya Jagannatha!; approved devotional-cultural history.

Book notes are not summaries alone. For each chapter record: (1) central claim, (2) evidence used, (3) assumptions or presuppositions, (4) a question or objection, and (5) one connection to another source.

Source-Status Labels

Throughout the course, source claims are labeled as follows:

  • [Mainstream] — Standard academic consensus or widely accepted scholarly interpretation
  • [Vedic] — Claims from Vedic scriptural, philosophical, or cultural traditions
  • [Creationist] — Arguments from creationist frameworks; represented accurately and compared with mainstream science
  • [Devotional] — Claims from devotional or community-source traditions; identified as such and compared with independent historical evidence
  • [Comparative] — Comparative hypotheses linking different traditions; assessed for evidence quality
